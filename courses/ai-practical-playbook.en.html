Module 01

How AI works

Four lessons on answer sources, invented information, missed constraints in long chats, and the differences between models, apps and tools.

Lesson 1.1

Where AI’s answers come from

By the end you can
  • Point to the source an answer came from.
  • Spot a number that has no source behind it, however confident the sentence sounds.
  • Turn an “empty” question into one that has a source.

A, an Account Executive, asks the company ChatGPT to write this week’s campaign summary email for a client. The first time, with nothing attached: the email is smooth and says “CTR up slightly from last week”. The second time, with the social listening export attached: the email sticks to real numbers and says “The file has no ad data, so I won’t comment on CTR.”

Same model, same question. “CTR up slightly” is the model guessing.

Three source groups to distinguish

  • ① Learned (pretrained knowledge): patterns the model learned from a huge amount of text, such as what share of voice is. The date where learning stops is the knowledge cutoff, for example June 2026.
  • ② Added by you (context): your question, attached files, earlier turns in the same chat. The company’s and the client’s data live here. A new chat does not automatically carry the whole old conversation; the app may still load instructions, Memory or connected sources.
  • ③ Tools it calls (tool): search the web, read files, run code, create images. They run only when switched on: web search cannot see a dashboard behind the client’s login page.
Good to know
  • The model cannot get into the social listening tool by itself. Gemini in Workspace can use Drive/Gmail when the relevant features, sources and permissions are enabled; a work account does not prove every source is connected.
  • Memory and Project keep some of it between chats (Lesson 2.2). Claude’s Memory default depends on your plan: check your settings.
Try it

Open a new chat on your company account, send round 1, then round 2 in the same chat.

Prompt · copy and adapt
ROUND 1:
How did social media conversation about brand X develop this week? Give me the 3 key numbers.

ROUND 2:
Here is the weekly data, exported from a social listening tool:
Day | Buzz  | Positive | Negative
Mon | 640   | 210      | 40
Tue | 1,200 | 380      | 130
Wed | 810   | 260      | 70
Thu | 590   | 200      | 45
Fri | 520   | 190      | 30
Answer the question above again, using only numbers from the table. For each number, say which row it came from. Anything the table cannot answer, mark MISSING.

Round 1 is vague, or has numbers with no source. Round 2 sticks to the table (buzz peaked Tuesday at 1,200) and has MISSING lines. That difference is source ②.

Apply it by team
▶BDE prepares a pitch brief for a prospective FMCG brand
  • Industry analysis framework: source ①, just ask.
  • Latest campaign: turn on web search (③) and ask for links.
  • Market share: find a public report through ③, or write “[no figure yet]”; don’t let the model fill it in.
▶HRF answers an employee’s question about leave policy
Without the policy, the AI answers from general practice (①), which may differ from company rules. Put the current policy file into the chat: “answer only from this file and quote the exact section; if the file doesn’t say, write that it doesn’t”.
Takeaway

Before you trust an answer, ask: which of the three sources did it come from? Do not use a number without traceable evidence.

Quick check

You ask the AI about the client’s campaign this week, with no file attached. It answers “CTR up 0.4 points, positive sentiment 68%”. What do you do with these two numbers?

Lesson 1.2

Why AI makes things up, and where

By the end you can
  • Explain why a model guesses instead of saying “I don’t know”.
  • Place a task in the low- or high-risk zone before handing it over.
  • Write a request that separates sourced numbers from estimates.

E, Business Development, drafts a pitch brief for a prospective nut milk brand and asks the AI about market share. The AI answers “this brand holds 23% market share”, with a link to a research firm’s page. The link opens, but the page has no 23%.

The brief goes out in the morning. In the afternoon the prospect asks: “Where does the 23% come from? Our internal figure is much lower.”

That is hallucination: the model states something that sounds certain but is not real. It lands in predictable places.

One driver of guessing is that training and evaluation can reward correct answers without rewarding appropriate abstention. As an analogy: on a 4-option question, a blank scores 0 and a random guess has an expected score of 0.25. This scoring encourages guessing under uncertainty. It is one mechanism, not the whole explanation; errors also depend on the model, data and task.

A confident answer is not evidence

Made-up and real numbers sound the same. A link only proves the page exists. For numbers, names, quotes, laws:

  1. Open the source.
  2. Find the exact sentence or table with the number, plus its scope and year.
  3. If you can’t find it, keep the number out of the report.
Asking in a way that invites a guess
  • “Give me brand X’s nut milk market share for 2025.”
Asking in a way that allows “I don’t know”
  • “If there is no source I can open, write NO SOURCE; you are allowed to say you don’t know. Give each number with a link and the verbatim quote that contains it; retract any number you cannot quote.”
Try it

Open a new chat; turn off web search if you can. Send round 1, then round 2 in the same chat.

Prompt · copy and adapt
ROUND 1:
Give me 5 key numbers about the [industry] market in Vietnam in 2025, each with a source.

ROUND 2:
For each number, organization name and report name above, state clearly:
(a) a specific source I can open and read, or
(b) an estimate from your trained knowledge.
You may answer "I don't know". Don't keep a number just because it sounds plausible.

Round 2 may expose unsupported items. A model’s self-report is not an independent check: still open the source and find the exact number.

Apply it by team
▶ResearchWriting observations from a brand health table
B, Research Manager, asks the AI to interpret a buzz and sentiment table. The AI often adds “15% above the industry average” though the table has no benchmark. Add to your prompt: “only compare with numbers in the table; no benchmark, no comparison”.
▶HRAsking about a new labor rule
F, HR, needs article numbers and effective dates: the AI easily gets these wrong. Get the original text from an official source, put it into the chat and require verbatim quotes.
Takeaway

AI may guess without evidence, so numbers, names, quotes and laws always need a source you can open.

Quick check

The AI writes “the nut milk market grew 18% in 2025”, with a link to a research firm’s page. Before putting it in a client report, what do you do?

Sources / further reading: OpenAI · Why language models hallucinate

Lesson 1.3

Why AI forgets more as a chat gets longer

By the end you can
  • Estimate how much room a document takes up in a chat.
  • Distinguish capacity limits from failures to use information in a long chat.
  • Move to a new chat without losing decisions already made.

D, a Planner, spends nearly two hours with the AI building a communication plan for a soft drink brand. In minute five they agree: never use the word “super”, no A-list KOL bookings because the budget is too small. By minute 110, the AI proposes the hook “super cool, super refreshing” with a round of A-list KOL bookings.

This error alone does not prove its desk, the context window, is full. The app may have compacted or truncated history, or the model may have missed a constraint still in context.

Tokens and desk size

  • Token: the unit of capacity, a text unit that may be part of a word, a whole word or punctuation; segmentation depends on the tokenizer. English is about 1.3 tokens per word.
  • Example: a 20-page English report, about 10,000 words, is about 13,000 tokens. On an illustrative 27,000-token desk, the document alone takes almost half; this is not a current product-plan limit.
  • Desk size: check the exact app, model and plan. App limits can differ from API specifications; check the account and model actually in use.
  • Hidden parts: system instructions, memory and reasoning also take up space, even though you never typed them.

When the desk is full, and even when it isn’t

When capacity is reached, the app may compact/cut history or report a limit. Automatic compaction in eligible claude.ai plans and features is one example (lesson 1.4). Depending on the app policy, some turns or details may no longer be sent to the model; the oldest part is not always what gets removed.

Even before capacity is reached, some model/task combinations perform worse with longer context, often called context rot; this is not a uniform or inevitable decline on every task. A decision buried among 80 rounds of trial and error is easy to miss.

Three desk-clearing habits

  1. Give each task its own chat, because old turns from the previous task still take up space.
  2. When the AI brings back a rejected direction, ask for a summary that pins the decisions down in one paragraph, then open a new chat.
  3. Only put what you need on the desk: the relevant pages of the brief instead of all 40.
Try it

Paste this prompt at the end of a long chat you are still working in. Check the entire agreed, rejected and open decision record against the approved brief and sources, including omissions; do not check only [?] items. Then transfer the checked summary and needed sources to a new chat.

Prompt · copy and adapt
Summarize the decisions in this chat in at most 10 lines so I can paste them into a new chat. Use 3 sections:
1. Agreed (with a short reason)
2. Rejected (with the reason it was rejected)
3. Still open
Only include what we actually agreed on. Wherever you are not sure whether something was agreed, mark it [?].
Apply it by team
▶Marketing / PlanningKeeping the brand voice across several planning sessions
Write the brand voice as a 5–8 line block: tone, banned words, rejected directions and why. Paste it at the top of every new chat; if you use it weekly, store it in a Project (Lesson 2.2).
Takeaway

Long chats may lose or miss constraints; pin your decisions down in one paragraph and open a new chat.

Quick check

After two hours of working on a content plan in one chat, the AI proposes a direction that was rejected in the first few turns. What should you do?

Sources / further reading: Liu et al. · Lost in the Middle (TACL 2024) · Chroma · Context Rot (technical report, 2025)

Lesson 1.4

How model, app and tools differ

By the end you can
  • Tell the model, the app and the tools apart.
  • Call on the right layer: code for calculations, web search for recent news, reasoning for tasks with many constraints.
  • Pick a mode with the “difficulty × freshness” grid.

B, Research Manager, pastes a 40-row GMV table and asks the AI for totals by category. Against Excel: two categories are wrong, one off by 38 million VND.

B asks again, adding: “Use the code tool to calculate this, and show me the result table.” This time it matches Excel exactly, because the AI got to use a tool.

Three layers

  • Model: the part that produces text, e.g. Claude Opus, GPT, Gemini.
  • App: what you open (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot). It decides which models you get.
  • Tools: web search, running code, reading files, creating images, connectors (let AI read the company’s email, Drive, CRM).

If a task needs a tool and none is called, switching to a pricier model won’t fix it.

Gemini on the company account

E, Business Development, sees Gemini in company Gmail and assumes it is the free personal-phone version. In fact this is the company account’s version.

  • Where it is: the side panel of Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, plus the Gemini app.
  • Languages: check per feature; side panels, AI functions, meeting notes and deck generation have different support lists.
  • Data: not reviewed by humans, not used to train models outside the domain without permission (lesson 5.1).
Before you paste company data

Look at the profile picture at the top right: a personal account falls under different terms (lesson 5.1). If a Gemini button is missing, ask IT before switching to a personal account.

Code execution and reasoning mode

A text model can solve arithmetic, but does not guarantee correct execution of an addition algorithm. For numeric tables, use a calculator or code so both input and calculation can be checked. Code execution (ChatGPT calls it Data analysis) lets the model run real code: the computer does the addition, and you can inspect the code and the intermediate table.

Reasoning mode has the model think through intermediate steps before answering: slower, but it holds many constraints. Use it for comparing options or planning around many constraints; rewriting an email does not need it (lesson 2.4). Some apps turn it on by themselves, some let you pick a thinking level.

Pick a mode by difficulty and freshness

In any box: if there is arithmetic on a table of numbers, call the code tool.

Try it

Send each round in a separate new chat.

Prompt · copy and adapt
ROUND 1 (new chat):
Calculate total GMV by category from the table below.

ROUND 2 (another new chat):
Use the code tool (Python) to calculate total GMV by category from the table below. Show the code, the result table by category, and the grand total.

Table (paste both times):
Category   | SKU | GMV (million VND)
Nut milk   | A1  | 148.6
Biscuits   | B1  | 92.3
Nut milk   | A2  | 77.9
Juice      | C1  | 63.4
Biscuits   | B2  | 118.7
Nut milk   | A3  | 205.2
Juice      | C2  | 49.8

Answer key: Nut milk 490.0; Biscuits 247.5; Juice 244.3; total 981.8 million VND. Round 1 may still be right, but only round 2 gives you an intermediate table to inspect.

Apply it by team
▶ResearchComparing two weeks of GMV across two marketplaces
Calculate in code, with an intermediate table by marketplace and by week, with units: confusing millions with billions shows up right away. Asking “why did it go up” needs promotion data; without it, the AI is only guessing.
▶BDA GMV → NMV → AOV → ROAS model
Do the calculations with the code tool, with a sanity check: If NMV means GMV minus nonnegative refunds/cancellations and deductions for the same period, NMV must not exceed GMV; check definitions first. For choosing between three pricing scenarios, use reasoning mode.
Takeaway

Use tools for calculations and ask AI to show inputs, code and the result table.

Quick check

The AI totals a 40-row table by category, and two categories differ from Excel. Which fix targets the right place?

Sources / further reading: OpenAI · reasoning best practices · Google · supported languages by feature

Module 02

Handing work to AI

Four lessons on handing work to AI the way you would to a new colleague: say what the task is, what to base it on, what shape the output takes and what counts as done; put the right papers on the desk; fix the right spot when it goes wrong; and know when AI needs to think longer.

Lesson 2.1

Write a 5-part brief instead of a one-line prompt

By the end you can
  • Write a 5-part brief for a task you repeat.
  • Point to the block that stops the AI adding its own reasons and commitments.
  • Use C.R.A.F.T as a secondary checklist, and a sample instead of a description.

A client asks A (Account Executive) why last week’s buzz was 1,200 in the old report and 1,050 in the new one. A types one line into an AI chat: “Write a professional email telling the client why this week’s number differs from last week’s.” The email comes back with two sentences nobody approved: “caused by a temporary system error” and “we will send the corrected figures before Friday”.

The real reason is in a note from the data team: the abnormal-user filter was just updated, so the old numbers were recalculated, and nobody has set a re-export date. Given one line, the AI fills the gaps with whatever sounds most plausible (Lesson 1.2). A brief (a work order) fills those gaps in advance.

1. The five parts of a brief

  • Task: what to do, for whom.
  • Context: what is going on, who will read it.
  • Source: which documents to rely on, and only those.
  • Output: format, length, structure.
  • Criteria: what counts as done, what to do when something is missing.

The test: hand the brief to a colleague who knows nothing about the task. If they would need to ask, the brief is incomplete; AI might ask or fill the gap, so check.

2. C.R.A.F.T is a secondary checklist

Common spelling: Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone. All of it falls into Task, Context and Output, with no place for Source and Criteria, exactly the two blocks that stopped the errors in Figure 2.1. A role adjusts tone but adds no facts.

The five-field template here is for a chat request. Work Agents adds action boundaries for delegated work; no field count is mandatory for every brief.

3. One sample instead of ten lines describing the tone

Instead of describing the tone, paste an old email the client responded well to and write “match the tone and length of the sample email, but take no figures from it”. This is few-shot prompting: the model imitates the sample.

Try it

Fill in the square brackets for an email you need to answer soon, and paste it into your company AI chat.

Prompt · copy and adapt
TASK: Write an email replying to a client who asked why [metric] this week differs from last week's report.

CONTEXT: The recipient is [job title], reads on a phone, and needs to know which number to use from now on.

SOURCE: Base this only on the note below. Do not add any other reason.
"""
[paste the note from the person in charge]
"""

OUTPUT: Email in [language], at most 120 words, 3 paragraphs: the reason, which number to use from now on, one next step. Match the tone and length of the sample email below, but take no figures from it.
"""
[paste a good past email]
"""

CRITERIA: Only mention dates and actions that are in the note, because the schedule is not set yet. If a new commitment is needed, write [TO CONFIRM]. After the email, list any sentences you could not find in the note.
Apply it by team
▶BDFollow-up email after a pitch
  • Source: the pitch notes, the approved price list.
  • Criteria: no prices, discounts or timelines outside the price list; unanswered questions get [FOLLOW UP].
▶ResearchAn observation paragraph from a brand health table
  • Source: the week’s buzz and sentiment score table.
  • Criteria: if two metrics rise together, write “rose together”, not “because”; no industry benchmark unless the file has one.
Takeaway

State the task, source and acceptance criteria so you can check the draft against them.

Quick check

The AI’s email gives the right reason for the discrepancy, but adds “the update will be ready before Friday”, which nobody has promised. How do you fix the prompt?

Sources / further reading: OpenAI · prompt/context optimization

Lesson 2.2

Give AI the right context

By the end you can
  • Pick the right papers: sample, source, facts.
  • Handle two conflicting documents.
  • Use Projects; know whether Memory is on or off.

F, from HR, needs a JD fast. F pastes 6 old JDs, a 40-page policy and an email thread into one chat. The draft includes “salary 12–15 million VND”: the 2023 range from an old JD, and an internal pay scale.

In this example, the model used an old document instead of the approved source. Long context can make source selection harder; adding a document does not necessarily lower accuracy.

1. Five kinds of paper

KindExample (JD)Where it goes
Facts of this task5 lines of role requirementsPaste into the chat

2. When two papers say different things

  1. Drop expired versions

    A newer version isn’t necessarily in force (a draft not yet issued).

  2. Say which paper wins

    “If the sample and the policy disagree, follow the 2026 policy.” Not sure? Ask the person in charge.

  3. Cut the extras

    Extract 2 pages instead of 40. If the decision is already in the requirements, drop the emails.

State the priority source

AI may choose the 2023 JD without flagging the conflict. State which version is in force.

Lesson 2.3

Find the real cause before fixing

By the end you can
  • Tell whether an error is in the source, reading, request or interpretation.
  • Write feedback that points only to the wrong sentence.
  • Know when to open a new chat.

A, Account Executive, asks the AI to write minutes of a client meeting, with an action list of owners and deadlines. The draft says “Dashboard handover: Oct 15”. A’s notes set no date, and the minutes are about to go to the client.

Adding “don’t make things up” or switching models fixes at most one cause. The wrong date can come from four places:

  • ① Not in the source: the notes have no date.
  • ② Misread: the source has a date, but for a different task.
  • ③ Request forces a fill-in: the prompt requires a deadline for every action.
  • ④ Conclusion goes too far: the source says “before mid-October”, the AI settles on Oct 15.

In branch ③, the request requires facts the source does not contain, so the request itself needs changing.

1. Find the branch in one minute

What you doWhat you seeBranch
Ctrl+F “15” in the notesNothing① or ③
Same as above“Oct 15” is there, but for a different task②
Search for “October”A close idea: “hope”, “expected”④
Reread your promptA required field the source has no data for③

2. Feedback that points to the wrong sentence

“Redo it” makes the AI change parts that were already right. Say which sentence is wrong, why (per the source), and what it should say.

Vague
  • “That’s wrong, redo it.”
  • “Stop making things up.”
Points to the spot
  • “The line ‘Dashboard handover: Oct 15’ is wrong. The notes only say the client wants it before mid-October. Change it to ‘Deadline: not agreed’ and update the follow-up email.”

3. When to open a new chat

Two or three fixes still miss, or the AI returns to an old error (Lesson 1.3):

  1. Ask the AI for a summary of what has been agreed.
  2. Open a new chat and paste the summary with the original notes.

4. Let the AI flag unsupported claims itself

  • Allow the AI to say “I don’t know”.
  • Require a source quote for each claim; no quote, retract the claim.
Try it

Paste this prompt into the chat where the AI just wrote your summary.

Prompt · copy and adapt
Before I check the draft above:

1. List every claim: dates, people's names, numbers, decisions, commitments.
2. For each claim, quote verbatim the supporting sentence from the original notes.
3. No supporting sentence: write NOT IN SOURCE and suggest a fix.
4. The original sentence only says something close (a wish, an estimate): write INTERPRETATION.

Return a table: Claim | Original sentence | Status. Do not give yourself a confidence percentage.
Apply it by team
▶ResearchSummarizing 500 comments on a TVC
  • ① An example comment that isn’t in the data: require its ID, drop any without one.
  • ③ The prompt says “exactly 5 topics”, so minor topics disappear: change it to “at most 5, list topics under 5% separately”.
▶Data“GMV rose thanks to the flash sale”
④ The file has no promotion data, yet the AI states a cause. Add a criterion: “only describe the size of the change; if data is missing, write PROMO DATA NEEDED”.
Takeaway

Before you fix the prompt, ask whether the error is in the source, the reading, the request or the interpretation; each has its own fix.

Quick check

The AI’s summary says: “The client has approved the Q4 budget.” Your notes say: “Q4 budget: awaiting CFO approval.” You have asked only once. What next?

Lesson 2.4

When to turn on reasoning mode

By the end you can
  • Recognize multistep or interacting-constraint tasks worth testing with reasoning.
  • Find reasoning mode in the app you use.
  • Ask for assumptions and basis instead of asking to see the thinking.

F, HR, needs an onboarding schedule for 6 new hires: 3 rooms, 4 sessions each, 2 mentors busy all day Tuesday. ChatGPT at Instant returns a schedule quickly but books mentor A on Tuesday and double-books a room. At High: a longer wait, no clashes, and a line at the bottom: “assuming mentor A is free Wednesday afternoon, needs confirming”.

Reasoning mode (thinking mode): the model works through intermediate steps before answering, so it is slower but can hold many constraints at once. Sums still need a calculation tool (Lesson 1.4).

Constraint count is only a clue: interactions, document difficulty and required precision matter. Summarizing 40 pages of contracts can be harder than comparing 12 simple cells. Use fast mode as a baseline, then compare the same tasks with reasoning.

1. Reasoning mode in each app

  • ChatGPT: check the mode/thinking-time selector in your account; names and levels depend on the plan, model and surface.
  • Claude: thinking may be automatic or configurable by model/surface; inspect the current interface and state all constraints.
  • Gemini: Thinking level, where your account has it. If not, break the task into smaller steps.

2. State goals and constraints, not “think step by step”

The model already reasons internally, so “think step by step” is unnecessary. State the goal and success criteria, then ask for the part you can review.

Asking to see the thinking
  • “Think very carefully, step by step.”
  • “Show me your whole thought process.”

Lesson 3.2

Check a source in 3 steps

By the end you can
  • Trace a claim back through three gates.
  • Spot when three links are really one piece of evidence.
  • Handle two sources with different numbers without averaging them.

B, Research Manager, presents a report to a brand. A slide says “42% of consumers prioritize same-day delivery”, and the client asks: “42% of whom?”

The source is a survey of 800 people in two cities, and the original line says: “ranked same-day delivery among the top 3 factors”. Real link, real number, but the slide claims more.

The way to catch it is to trace back: go from the claim to the exact sentence, the exact table, in the original source.

1 · Primary source and secondary source

  • Primary source: where the number was created, for example a survey report or an official document.
  • Secondary source: an article that quotes it.
Many matching pages are not many sources

Five pages with the same number may be one original line copied five times, or no original line at all.

2 · Three gates for tracing back

  1. Access the document

    Check that you can read its contents; a URL alone is insufficient.

  2. Does the source say that exact thing?

    A link can be real while its content does not support the claim.

  3. Do the scope and date still match?

    Who was sampled, how many, where, when.

Every number sent to a client carries source, period and the relevant denominator/sample size. Example: “42% of respondents ranked same-day delivery among their top 3 factors ([name] survey, 2025, 800 people, 2 cities)”.

3 · Ranking sources

TierIncludesHow to use it
Tier 1original research reports, official company pages, government portalscheck definitions, method and scope before using numbers
Tier 2mainstream press, industry blogs with a named author, Wikipediaa starting point; for numbers, trace back to Tier 1
Tier 3SEO pages, AI-written blogs, unsourced news, social media comments, disguised PRfor ideas, not for numbers

4 · Two sources, two numbers

Two reports that differ by almost 2x may measure two different things (GMV or net revenue, every channel or only the big marketplaces). Compare definition, scope, year; if they differ, keep both and state what each number measures.

Lesson 3.4

Spot and fix AI slop

By the end you can
  • Spot 12 signs of AI slop, including formatting slop.
  • Hand off writing: facts first, words second, with a sample.
  • Have AI check itself against a ban list.

A, Account Executive, asks AI to write a launch caption for an insulated bottle and gets back: “Not just a bottle — an experience. Bold. Refined. Different.” Below it: five bold bullets, one sentence per line, closing with “Hope this caption helps! 🚀”. The manager asks: what does the bottle do and what does it cost?

That is AI slop: grammatical, smooth writing that carries no information and still bears the marks of a chat window.

1 · Twelve signs, four groups

A sign is a cue to edit, not proof; AI text detectors are not proof either. Punctuation, emoji and transitions do not identify authorship. The list below is an editing convention for this exercise, not an AI detector or a rule for every genre.

SignSlop exampleFix it as
A · Words
① Adjective triplet, hype words“Bold. Refined. Different.”One fact: “Double-wall steel, ice lasts till afternoon.”
② Mechanical transitions“Furthermore…”, “In conclusion…”Delete them.
B · Sentence patterns
③ “Not just X, but Y”“Not just a bottle, but a style statement.”“Comes in 3 colors, free engraving.”
④ Stock opener“In today’s fast-moving market…”“500 ml bottle, 290,000 VND.”
⑤ Stock closer, leftover chat talk“Hope this caption helps!”, “Would you like me to…?”Delete; end with the action to take.
C · Formatting
⑥ Line break after every sentence15 paragraphs, one sentence each.Group related sentences into 2-3 sentence paragraphs.
⑦ Bullets for everything“- Quality: superior. - Design: refined.”Lists only for real lists.
⑧ Bold everywhereFour bold phrases in a five-line paragraph.At most one per paragraph.
⑨ Heading and emoji over a two-sentence paragraph“## 🚀 Key Benefits”No heading under three paragraphs; no emoji.
⑩ Em dash (—) joining clauses“An insulated bottle — your everyday companion.”Comma, colon, or a new sentence.
D · Content
⑪ Sentence with no fact, empty promise“Everyone will love it right away.”“Return within 7 days.”
⑫ Vague attribution“Experts say…”Source and number: “Survey of 120 customers: 68%.”

2 · How to fix it

  1. Facts first, words second. List five real facts: a number, a feature, a price, a deadline, one customer quote. Write [MISSING FACT] where one is lacking.
  2. Give a voice sample and a layout sample. Paste 2-3 real passages; AI copies the paragraphing too.
  3. A short ban list, with what to do instead. “No bullets; write 2-3 sentence paragraphs.”
  4. Let AI check itself, you read last.
Try it
Prompt · copy and adapt
Step 1. Facts: [paste 5 real facts]. List them back, adding nothing.

Step 2. Write [a 3-sentence caption] for [the task].
- Each sentence uses at least 1 fact; if one is missing, write [MISSING FACT].
- Match the voice and layout of this sample: [paste 1 real passage].
- Paragraphs of 2-3 sentences. No line break after every sentence, no bullets, no bold, no emoji, no headings.
- Banned: adjective triplets; "not just… but"; rhetorical questions; em dashes (—); "Furthermore", "In conclusion"; "Hope this helps"; "Experts say"; "Here is".

Step 3. Flag every violation and every sentence with no fact.
Takeaway

For this caption, prioritize checkable facts; transitions and creative language still have a place when they help the reader.

Lesson 4.2

Numbers: ask for the denominator, let tools calculate

By the end you can
  • Ask three questions before trusting a number.
  • Tell a % increase from a percentage-point increase; catch millions mixed with billions.
  • Make AI calculate with a tool and show the intermediate table.

B, Research Manager, compares two weeks on a marketplace: week A 1,000 views, 100 orders; week B 1,200 views, 120 orders. The AI writes for the slide: "Orders rose 20%, conversion rate improved sharply."

The second half is wrong: conversion was 10% in both weeks (100 ÷ 1,000, 120 ÷ 1,200). Arithmetically, orders rose in proportion to views in this example. This table does not establish the cause or page quality.

  • Conversion rate: out of every 100 people who view, how many place an order.
  • Denominator: the thing you divide by. You need the denominator to interpret a rate correctly.

When the AI gives you only one chart, ask: what does the other chart look like?

Three questions before trusting a number

AI writesRealityQuestion
"Conversion rose sharply"Orders +20%, rate 10% → 10%What is the denominator?
Translation: "positive share up 18%"40% → 58%: up 18 points, which is +45%Definition: points or percent?
"Total GMV across two marketplaces: 851.2"850 (million) + 1.2 (billion) = 2.05 billionDefinition: what unit is each column?
"Marketplace 2 grew fastest"The file only has one monthCompared with which period?
The 10-second sum: does it add up to 100%?

If the three marketplaces are disjoint and cover all revenue, their shares should total about 100%. A sum of 48% + 39% + 20% = 107% calls for checking denominators, overlap, formulas and errors; it does not identify one cause. For every table the AI returns: add up the total, check the units, check the time period.

Calculation belongs to the tools layer (lesson 1.4), for example Claude's code execution or ChatGPT's Data analysis. Write it straight into the request: "use a tool to calculate, no mental math, show an intermediate table with the denominator of each rate".

Safety, agents and team reuse

Which data can go into which tool; what permissions to give AI when it takes many steps on its own; and how to turn a good way of working into something the whole team reuses.

Lesson 5.1

Check data before it goes into AI

By the end you can
  • Sort a file into one of three data colors.
  • Pick the right account type for each color.
  • Distinguish replacing names with codes from data that no longer identifies anyone.

B, Research Manager, needs to QC sentiment labels on 3,000 mentions that still carry posters' names and the client's brand names. B pastes the whole file into a personal ChatGPT account: done in 20 minutes, but identifiable data has left anything the company controls.

Replacing names with codes does not establish permission to process the file. Even a work account needs an approved purpose, minimum necessary data and the organization’s procedure. The risk sits in the pairing: which kind of data, which kind of account.

This is an exercise workflow policy, not a legal classification for every company. Each tool still needs approval for the purpose and data involved; green does not replace usage rights.

Find the file's color, then move across to the account you have open. The bottom strip has no exceptions.

1 · Three steps before you paste

  1. Guess the damage

    If this showed up in tomorrow's news, who would be hurt?

    • Users or clients: red (mentions with names, CVs, payroll).
    • Only the company, embarrassed: yellow.
    • Nobody: green.

  2. Reduce identifiability

Lesson 5.2

Give agents just enough permission

By the end you can
  • Say how an agent differs from a regular chat, and name the agent in each of the three apps.
  • Choose the lowest permission step that still gets a multi-step job done.
  • Put a human approver before every action that cannot be undone.

10 p.m., a clip trashing a product from brand X starts spreading. A, an Account Executive, turns on an agent: "read the last 6 hours of mentions, draft a crisis alert and email it to the client". 12 minutes later the client gets an alert saying "40 independent complaints establish a crisis"; in fact 30 groups reshared the same post. The content appeared in 40 groups, but group reach is not the number of independent complaints; no agreed crisis criterion has been established.

The draft confused dissemination with independent evidence. Granting sending rights let that unchecked conclusion go straight to the client.

An agent is AI that takes multiple steps in your tools on its own: read, draft, edit, send, delete. What you control is least privilege: the lowest step that still finishes the job, nothing extra "just in case".

This is an exercise permission ladder, not an undo guarantee: reads can leak data, and edits need backups and correct scope. The course reserves sending, payments and permanent deletion for a person.

1 · Agents in each app

  • ChatGPT: ChatGPT Work, depending on your plan.
  • Claude: Claude Cowork, works on files in the folder you grant.
  • Gemini: the Gemini task feature discussed here needs AI Pro/Ultra and a personal account; this restriction does not apply to every Gemini feature.

Connector permissions (email, Drive, CRM) change over time. Before turning one on, check in your own account: can it only read, or also write?

2 · If it gets tricked, what can it do?

Agents read emails written by other people; if one contains a command, the agent may follow it (prompt injection, lesson 5.1). So set limits in permissions, not in instructions.

  • Steps 1–3: open only the sources and folders it needs.
  • Step 4 (send, buy, delete, sign in): a person confirms.
  • Agent doing something odd: stop the session right away.

3 · When an agent is worth it

  • Yes: repeated work, across several files, with a checkable result (totals match the source file).
  • No: one-off work that needs judgment at every turn. Use chat.

Lesson 5.3

Package your workflow for the team

By the end you can
  • Package a workflow into 5 parts so others can rerun it.
  • Pick where to store it: Project, Gem or Agent Skills.
  • Measure before and after, and know when to automate.

A, an Account Executive, cut the weekly report from 3 hours to 50 minutes with a Project holding a solid brief. A colleague asks for "the prompt", gets one line, and produces a report with a wrong total buzz. A's sample report, source file and checklist were not in that line.

What you need to share is the package: five parts that let someone else get an equivalent result without asking A.

1 · The five parts

  • Brief template: the 5-part brief (lesson 2.1) that has run for real, weekly changes in [square brackets].
  • Priority sources: which file is the source of the numbers, which file wins when they disagree.
  • Good example: a report you already sent, client name removed.
  • Checklist: e.g. "total buzz matches the export file".
  • Limits: e.g. "don't explain rises or drops when promotion data is missing".

2 · Where to store it

  • Project (ChatGPT, Claude): easiest place to start (lesson 2.2).
  • Gem (Gemini): usable during the transition to Skills; check the timeline for your company account.
  • Agent Skills: a folder with a SKILL.md file. Available in Claude, some ChatGPT plans; check in your account.
  • Custom GPT: migration to plugins is planned; standard retirement is December 11, 2026, with February 11, 2027 for FedRAMP or approved deferrals. Personal accounts cannot create new GPTs; check the workspace notice before choosing storage.

Skills began rolling out across Workspace on November 9, 2026. Company Gems remain usable during the transition; full replacement is no sooner than March 1, 2027 for Business/Enterprise or June 1 for Education. Do not apply personal-account dates to company accounts or assume Skills already have every Gem sharing/file feature. Google · product-specific transition.

A skill downloaded from outside: IT approves it first, then you use it.

3 · Measure before and after

  • One run = prep + run + check + fix.
  • Before: 180 minutes by hand.
  • After: 10 + 5 + 20 + 15 = 50 minutes.

Don't skip the check to make the number look better. Three passes are this lesson’s trial convention for low-risk work; also test missing data/revoked access and retest the final version. Automation needs risk-appropriate acceptance and run monitoring; a person still sends (lesson 5.2).
